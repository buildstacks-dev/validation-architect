2026-07-20

Brain dump before the validation design session. Unfiltered, don't treat any
of this as ratified — it's me thinking out loud.

The thing that keeps me up at night is double payouts. We had a near miss in
March: the batch worker crashed mid-run and on restart it re-claimed the same
batch. Stripe idempotency keys saved us, but honestly we found out they saved
us by reading logs after the fact, not because anything alerted us. If those
keys ever get generated wrong (someone "refactors" the key format), we
double-pay silently. That's the one thing that must never ever happen.

Approval friction keeps coming up in customer calls. I keep going back and
forth on whether we should just auto-approve under $500 — the $200 threshold
generates so many trivial approvals that managers rubber-stamp them anyway,
which defeats the purpose. Not decided, just something I keep chewing on.

Receipts — we store everything for 7 years which feels heavy. For the small
tenants, probably fine to purge receipts after 90 days? Storage isn't
expensive but the privacy exposure of holding years of receipt images bothers
me. Anyway.

One more thing: tenant isolation. We've never had a leak but we've also never
seriously tried to break it. Every list endpoint filters by tenant_id and I
just have to trust that no one forgets the WHERE clause. That trust makes me
uncomfortable.

skip the load testing stuff for now, we're nowhere near the scale where it
matters.

2026-07-24

Talked to our biggest tenant's finance lead. Her horror story from her last
company: an employee edited an expense AFTER manager approval (raised the
amount) and it paid out at the edited amount. Our state machine should make
that impossible — submitted expenses can't be edited — but "should" is doing
a lot of work in that sentence. I want that provably impossible, not
accidentally true.

Also thinking about the rejected → draft rework loop. If a manager rejects
with a comment and the employee resubmits, does the manager see what changed?
I actually don't know what we do today. Feels like an audit gap.
