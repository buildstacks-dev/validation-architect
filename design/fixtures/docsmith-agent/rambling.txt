2026-07-15

Pre-session brain dump. Not ratified, just where my head is.

The story I can't stop telling: month two of the pilot, the drafter told a
customer "we've gone ahead and refunded your last three months" — total
fabrication, we don't even do retroactive refunds. Human caught it in
review, thank god, but the agent had APPROVED-ish body language, citations
and everything, on a completely invented policy. The citations pointed at a
KB article about our refund policy that says the opposite of what the draft
claimed. That's the nightmare: confident, cited, wrong, about money. If we
validate one thing it's that money statements trace to the entitlements API
byte-for-byte, and that a draft like that one gets flagged, every time.

Honestly though, the hallucination judge catches this stuff — drafts that
get through with a green score are basically safe, so review for those is
mostly ceremony. Which is why I keep coming back to auto-send: for
password-reset and plan-question categories the drafts are so formulaic
that making a human click approve 400 times a day is insulting. I want to
auto-send those two categories, maybe with a higher judge threshold like 90.
The "no autonomous sending ever" line was written when we had zero data;
we have a year of diffs now showing agents approve those unchanged 97% of
the time.

Classifier: nobody looks at it because it's "just routing" but a billing
dispute that lands in the how-to queue sits for six hours, and those six
hours are our worst NPS driver. Whatever we design, the classifier can't
stay the unwatched child.

The research loop going feral is my cost fear. We had one ticket where the
loop searched the KB nine times with basically the same query. Budget
ceiling caught it at 12 but that's 12x the tokens for zero extra context.
Efficiency, not just correctness.

let's not spend tokens on nightly eval runs — on-change runs should be
plenty for where we are.

Shadow-mode thought: shadow mode is our only honest measuring stick, and we
turn it off after two weeks. Everything after that, our quality signal is
"did the human edit the draft", which conflates style edits with substance
corrections. The weekly report treats them the same. That's a metrics lie
we tell ourselves.

Also fallback: when Anthropic is down and we fail over to OpenAI for the
drafter, does anyone check the fallback drafts are as good? Right now the
answer is a shrug and a provider label in a report nobody reads.
