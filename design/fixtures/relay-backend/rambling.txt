2026-07-18

Thoughts before the validation design work. Raw, not ratified.

The pitch I keep giving customers is "we guarantee exactly-once delivery to
subscribers" and it works great in sales calls. That's the promise I want
the validation to protect above everything.

The thing I actually lose sleep over is the dispatcher failover. The whole
design says the in-memory schedule is "just a cache" and the standby rebuilds
from Postgres. We've tested that exactly once, manually, a year ago. Every
incident review since then has had someone ask "did the failover double-send
or stall anything?" and the honest answer is we look at graphs and shrug. I
want failover to be something we can trigger in a test a hundred times and
prove nothing is lost, nothing stalls, and duplicates stay within the
documented window.

Head-of-line blocking: right call for ordering, but one dead subscriber
endpoint holding up a whole stream is our #1 support ticket. The manual-skip
escape hatch exists but I don't think anyone's ever exercised it against a
production-shaped queue. Also what happens if an admin skips an event and
then replays the DLQ — does the skipped event come back? I genuinely don't
know. That scares me.

DLQ replay at 3am: when a big subscriber outage ends, whoever is on call
should be able to replay the DLQ fast. Requiring tenant-admin auth for
replay feels heavy in that moment — our on-call is usually a developer role.
Maybe replay should be developer-level? Not decided. There's a real tension
between "3am speed" and "replay re-sends real traffic".

Secret rotation is the feature I'd bet money has a bug. Dual-signing
windows, config-driven expiry, per-endpoint state — it has "timer edge case"
written all over it. Nobody has ever rotated a secret under load.

don't bother with the multi-region story, that's a next-year problem.

2026-07-23

One more: retention compaction. The compactor moves events to S3 after 30
days. If compaction races a late DLQ replay (replaying an event that just
got compacted), what happens? Either answer is defensible but SOMETHING
defined better happen, not a 500 and a shrug.

Also the meta-webhook (alerting webhook that fires on DLQ threshold) — it's
delivered through Relay itself. If Relay is sick, the thing telling you
Relay is sick is also sick. Chuckled when I wrote it, but it's real.
